Persopy builds AI personas of experts and historical figures from the full record
those people left behind. A prompt can imitate a voice; the harder problem is
having the underlying corpus organised well enough that an agent can reach it,
cite it, and know the limits of what it supports.

## The Problem

Any language model will happily answer "as Einstein" if you ask it to. What it
cannot do reliably is tell you which of its claims come from a paper the person
actually wrote, which come from what others later wrote about them, and which
are confabulated to fill a gap.

For education, research, and cultural memory, that distinction is the whole
point.

## What a Persopy Persona Does

- **Answers from sources.** Responses are drawn from the actual documents, not
  from a model's memory of them. The persona can point to the passage it used.
- **Separates what is whose.** A person's own words are never blended with
  later commentary about them. The persona keeps the difference and will say
  which it is speaking from.
- **Stops where the record stops.** Where the documents are silent or
  uncertain, it says so—in character, without inventing memories, quotations,
  or opinions.

## Where It Applies

**Memory and testimony.** Recorded testimony rendered as a persona that answers
in the first person, strictly within what the witness documented—so visitors and
students can ask their own questions, and the account stays reachable for the
next generation.

**Research institutes.** A lab's own publication record, distilled into the
methods behind it, so students and collaborators can consult the group's
accumulated reasoning directly.

**Historical figures.** Complete primary works—papers, letters, notebooks—
reconstructed into an interlocutor for study, teaching, and public engagement.
